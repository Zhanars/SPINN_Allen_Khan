## 1. The three works cited by the reviewers

### 1.1 Xia, Böttcher, Chou (Reviewer 1, major (i), first half) [C][A][T]

* Xia, M.; Böttcher, L.; Chou, T. Spectrally adapted physics-informed neural networks for solving unbounded domain problems. *Mach. Learn.: Sci. Technol.* **2023**, 4(2), 025024. DOI 10.1088/2632-2153/acd0a1. arXiv:2202.02710 (v1 6 Feb 2022, v2 28 Feb 2023). The reviewer's citation (4(2), 025024, 2023) is correct.
* **What the network does.** Input is time only; space enters only through fixed basis functions. Output is the vector of time-dependent spectral coefficients w_i(t; Θ), u_N(x,t) = Σ w_i(t) φ_i(x). This is architecturally the same idea as our Eq. (spectral-pinn).
* **Bases.** Modified mapped Gegenbauer functions (function-fitting example), **Chebyshev polynomials on (−1,1)** (Example 2, bounded-domain control), generalized Laguerre on R+, generalized Hermite on R, R², R³. Adaptivity: scaling β, translation x_L, and p-adaptive order N; hyperbolic-cross index set in 2D/3D. No Fourier basis in the method.
* **Domain.** Mostly unbounded (the title topic); one bounded Chebyshev example.
* **Derivatives.** Spatial derivatives explicit from the basis (cost O(N) for all orders, claimed advantage over autodiff). Time: implicit Runge–Kutta stage values with residuals summed in the loss (their Eq. 9), one parameter set per time window; the text also makes a general remark about autodiff in t that is not used in the Eq. 9 formulation. So their time handling differs from ours (single network over all of [0,T], dc/dt by autodiff, collocation in t).
* **Loss terms.** Function-fitting MSE; RK-stage residual in L²(x); Dirichlet boundary penalty (Laguerre example); inverse-problem terms; L² regularisation for source recovery.
* **PDEs.** All linear: a first-order transport-type equation (Chebyshev example), 2D/3D heat, 1D Schrödinger, heat with source. **No Allen–Cahn, no nonlinear PDE, no energy/Lyapunov structure.**
* **Inverse problems.** Yes: unknown diffusivity κ in the heat equation from noisy data (windowed estimation) and source reconstruction (L-curve for the regularisation weight). Larger noise degrades results; late-time spreading causes difficulties.
* **Comparisons / stress tests.** Standard PINN vs s-PINN for the Chebyshev example (about 10^6 s vs 1711 s adaptive and 1008 s non-adaptive, as reported by the authors); Crank–Nicolson comparison; table on output count with hyperbolic cross; network depth/width sweep (narrow or shallow networks failed to converge). No mode-count-at-fixed-budget scaling study, no long-time study, no symmetry, no formal verification.
* **Stated limitations.** Need to know asymptotics to choose the basis; time still stepped; output count grows exponentially with dimension; noise sensitivity.
* **Consequence for us.** The reviewer is right that "network outputs spectral coefficients, derivatives exact from the basis, inverse problem from noisy data, Chebyshev basis" is **not novel**. Our manuscript does not cite it; it must be cited and the Contributions (i)-(ii) reworded. What differs: Allen–Cahn (nonlinear gradient flow), whole-interval single network with autodiff in time rather than windowed implicit RK, Chebyshev with Neumann on [−1,1] as the main case, energy penalty, 2D reflection-symmetric Fourier variant, systematic negative results.

### 1.2 Kütük and Yücel (Reviewer 1, major (i), second half) [C][A][T]

* Kütük, M.; Yücel, H. Energy dissipation preserving physics informed neural network for Allen–Cahn equations. *J. Comput. Sci.* **2025**, 87, 102577 (May 2025). DOI 10.1016/j.jocs.2025.102577. arXiv:2411.08760 (v1 13 Nov 2024, v2 12 Mar 2025). The reviewer's citation is correct.
* **Network.** Standard coordinate PINN: input (x,t), output û(x,t) directly; ResNet with tanh (about 4×100 in 1D, 6×128 in 2D/3D); all derivatives by autodiff; Adam then L-BFGS; Latin-hypercube points; residual-based adaptive sampling and a temporal-adaptivity algorithm (a new network per time segment). **No spectral coefficients are output.** Fourier series appear only to build a continuous analogue of random initial data.
* **Energy penalty.** Weak, one-sided, squared: loss_e = mean over n_e points of [max(0, ∂E/∂t(û))]², with ∂E/∂t obtained by autodiff (continuous-time, not a discrete checkpoint difference). Weight λ_e is reported per example (the 2D polynomial case reads (λ_r, λ_b, λ_i, λ_e) = (1, 1, 1000, 5200) in the version we read [verify]); the text we read gives no rule for choosing them. The authors describe the constraint as weak, i.e., no guarantee of decay.
* **PDEs / cases.** Allen–Cahn with constant or degenerate mobility, polynomial or logarithmic potentials, 1D, 2D, 3D, deterministic and random initial data, advection. The 1D case is u0 = x² cos(πx) on [−1,1], T = 1, quartic potential, **periodic** BCs, as read from the HTML [verify].
* **Maximum bound principle.** Checked numerically (solution stays in [−1,1], or [−s,s] for the logarithmic case); no proof, not enforced.
* **Evidence.** Quantitative error only for the 1D case against a Chebfun reference (relative l2 about 0.0078 without and 0.0053 with the energy penalty, in the table we read [verify]); other cases qualitative; single runs, no seeds, no timing comparison with a classical solver; CPU, TensorFlow 1.14, float32.
* **Not present.** Inverse problem, symmetry, formal verification, spectral output layer.
* **Consequence for us.** "Penalise energy growth in a PINN loss for Allen–Cahn" is **not novel**. Differences that are real but modest: our penalty acts on the checkpointed energy increments E(τ_{i+1}) − E(τ_i) of the coefficient network, with exact spectral u_x in the energy; theirs on dE/dt through autodiff. Our measured effect of the penalty (identical accuracy and zero violations with and without it) is the opposite of a benefit and should be stated as such. Note also that the benchmark IC x² cos(πx) appears here with periodic BCs, which is the origin of the Neumann inconsistency flagged by Reviewer 1 (ii): the same IC is the Raissi et al. benchmark (Allen–Cahn example in the discrete-time section of arXiv:1711.10561, with periodic BCs on [−1,1]) [T].

### 1.3 He and Pan (Reviewer 1, minor (ii)) [C], full text [X]

* He, D.; Pan, K. Maximum norm error analysis of an unconditionally stable semi-implicit scheme for multi-dimensional Allen–Cahn equations. *Numer. Methods Partial Differ. Equ.* **2019**, 35(3), 955–975. DOI 10.1002/num.22333 (online 6 Dec 2018; print May 2019).
* **Abstract (Crossref):** a linearised finite-difference scheme, modified leap-frog in time, nonlinear term semi-implicit, central differences in space; the scheme satisfies a discrete energy decay property and is unconditionally stable; second-order maximum-norm error estimate; 2D/3D tests.
* **Not verifiable.** The publisher page returns HTTP 403, no arXiv version exists (arXiv API and web search), the author's faculty page lists no PDF. The reviewer's description ("severe numerical growth despite decreasing modified energy") is **not** contained in the abstract and could not be checked. Recommendation: cite it only for what the abstract states (a scheme with a discrete energy decay property and max-norm error analysis), and ground the statement "energy decay alone does not guarantee admissible (bounded) solutions" in the canonical maximum-bound-principle (MBP) references in §2.2 (Tang–Yang 2016, Shen–Tang–Yang 2016, Du–Ju–Li–Qiao 2021), whose content is MBP theory for Allen–Cahn-type equations. In the response letter say honestly that the full text was not accessible.

## 4. Positioning paragraph (draft, about 200 words, English, journal style)

> **Positioning with respect to prior work.** Several ingredients of the present approach have precedents and are not claimed as new. Representing the solution by a network that receives only time and returns the coefficients of a fixed spectral basis, with spatial derivatives taken exactly from the basis, was introduced for bounded (Chebyshev) and unbounded domains, together with parameter estimation from noisy data, by Xia et al. [ref_Xia2023]; related coefficient-space networks have since been proposed [ref_Du2024, ref_Bizzi2025]. Adding a one-sided penalty on energy growth to a PINN loss for the Allen–Cahn equation was proposed by Kütük and Yücel [ref_Kutuk2025], and scalar-auxiliary-variable networks guarantee discrete dissipation by construction [ref_Zhang2024, ref_Lin2025]. Imposing boundary conditions or symmetries exactly through the architecture is likewise established [ref_Dong2021, ref_Sukumar2022, ref_Lu2021]. The Lean 4 results formalize the classical energy identity for exact gradient flows and make no statement about a trained network. Our contribution is therefore empirical: a controlled, multi-seed comparison, for the Allen–Cahn equation, of a single time-only Chebyshev-coefficient network with a space–time PINN and a classical Chebyshev collocation solver, reported together with its failure modes (loss of accuracy as the mode count grows at matched budget, a reproducible bias in the recovered interface parameter, and an energy penalty that is inactive once the residual is small), and a demonstration that restricting the Fourier basis enforces reflection symmetry to machine precision. We do not claim to outperform classical spectral solvers [ref_Grossmann2024].

## 5. Points that bear directly on the reviewer comments (not literature, check before use)

1. **Reviewer 1 (iii), connecting the Lean theorem to the trained network (own derivation).** Let r := u_t − (ε² u_xx − f(u)) be the PDE residual of the network output. Because ∇E(u) = −ε² u_xx + f(u) (Neumann BCs), u_t = −∇E + r and hence d/dt E(u) = −‖u_t‖² + ⟨r, u_t⟩ + ε² [u_x u_t]_{x=−1}^{x=1}, so d/dt E ≤ −½‖u_t‖² + ½‖r‖² + (boundary term). The boundary term vanishes only if the network satisfies u_x(±1,t) = 0 exactly, which a truncated Chebyshev series does not (only through the BC penalty). The abstract Hilbert-space lemma already formalised in Lean extends to "c' = −∇E(c) + r implies (E∘c)' = −‖c'‖² + ⟨r, c'⟩", a one-line change, and gives a computable certificate: the integrated squared residual and boundary term bound the possible energy increase. This is the same type of stability-based reasoning used for PINN error estimates in [ref_Mishra2023]. It would turn the appendix from a statement about exact flows into a statement that a small training residual implies bounded energy growth.
2. **Reviewer 1 minor (ii), MBP.** The exact solution satisfies |u| ≤ 1 for |u0| ≤ 1 (here |x² cos πx| ≤ 1) [ref_Du2021b, ref_Tang2016]. A truncated Chebyshev series can overshoot (Gibbs-type effects), so report max over (x,t) of |u_θ| for the experiments; the only learning-based method we found that enforces the bound architecturally is the clamp in [ref_Geng2025].
3. **Reviewer 1 (ii), initial condition.** The IC x² cos(πx) is the standard PINN benchmark with *periodic* BCs (Raissi et al.; Kütük–Yücel). u_x(−1,0) = 2 and u_x(1,0) = −2, so it is inconsistent with the Neumann condition used here; the reviewer is correct.
4. **Reviewer 2 comment 4 (niche).** For single forward solves the literature outcome is that classical solvers win [ref_Grossmann2024, ref_McGreivy2024]; the plausible niches in the coefficient-network literature are parametric/repeated solves [ref_Du2024], long-horizon prediction [ref_Lin2025, ref_Bizzi2025], differentiable inverse problems [ref_Xia2023], and embedding of exact structure. Do not claim more than the new experiments support.
5. **Reviewer 2 comment 5 and 2 (loss weighting, λ_E).** [ref_Wang2021a] and [ref_Rathore2024] are, judging by title and the abstract summary available to us, the relevant references for loss-term imbalance and ill-conditioning of the residual; Kütük–Yücel report per-case weights without a selection rule, so a λ_E ablation would go beyond the closest prior work.
