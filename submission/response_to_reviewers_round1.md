## A. Conclusions of the first version that we withdraw

1. **"Accuracy degrades as the number of modes K grows" (optimization bottleneck).** This was an implementation artefact. In our code the Neumann penalty acted on u_x(±1)/K² and was then squared, so its effective weight decayed like K⁻⁴; the trained K=64 network had wall slopes of up to ≈4–6 instead of 0. With an exact Neumann basis (Section 2.3, Eq. 8) the K=64 error is 1.5·10⁻³ instead of 9.9·10⁻² (Section 3.2, Table 2, Figure 2); a correctly rescaled soft penalty has the same effect. **TODO: add the K=64 rescaled-penalty and K=128/256 numbers from Table 2.**
2. **"A robust ≈38 % overestimation bias in the recovered ε".** It is the footprint of the starting value of the optimizer, not a property of the estimator: with joint Adam training the recovered value is ≈0.7 × ε_init for every starting value and every ε_true tested (Section 3.5, Table 6, Figure 5a). The numbers of the first version for noisy data and for the single run at ε_true = 0.05 came from a script whose settings differed from those stated and were not archived; they are withdrawn. We replaced the estimator (variable projection, Section 2.6) and compared it with classical least squares, including the question of identifiability (Table 7, 8).
3. **"Zero energy violations are obtained by the energy-constrained loss."** The unpenalized network has no violations either, and the penalty term is positive in at most 1 of 6000 epochs (Section 3.3, Table 4, Figure 3).

We also found, by an independent audit of the 77 numerical statements of the first version against the stored data, that 22 were wrong and 7 could not be verified (details in the table at the end). Among them: the Chebyshev row of Table 1 was computed in a different norm than the PINN rows, and the finite-difference reference had first-order wall rows. All numbers were recomputed against a certified spectral reference (Appendix E).

## B. Reviewer 1

> **Major (i).** Spectral–PINN hybridization (Xia et al., MLST 2023) and energy-dissipation penalization (Kütük & Yücel, J. Comput. Sci. 2025) are not novel.

You are right, and we were not aware of these papers. We have verified both (bibliographic data, abstracts and, for the first, the full text), cite them (Introduction, paragraph 3; Section 2.3, 2.4) and removed all claims of novelty for (a) networks of time alone outputting spectral coefficients with exact spatial derivatives (Xia et al. do this, including a Chebyshev basis on (−1,1) and an inverse problem for a diffusivity; related: Du et al. 2024, Bizzi et al. 2025) and (b) a one-sided energy penalty for Allen–Cahn (Kütük & Yücel; their penalty acts on dE/dt through automatic differentiation, ours on checkpoint increments). We also cite energy-dissipative neural solvers built on the SAV approach (Zhang et al. 2024; Lin et al. 2025), the limiter of Geng et al. 2025 for the maximum bound, hard-constraint architectures, and the established finding that classical solvers beat PINNs on forward problems (Grossmann et al. 2024; McGreivy & Hakim 2024). The revised Introduction ends with an explicit "Scope and contributions" paragraph which states what is *not* new and lists the contributions we do claim: (i) the exact Neumann basis and the diagnosis of the penalty artefact, (ii) a controlled multi-seed evaluation against a certified reference and the classical Chebyshev solver on three benchmarks, (iii) the a-posteriori energy balance (Lean-verified) evaluated for every trained network, (iv) an ablation of the energy penalty, (v) a diagnosis and a correction of the inverse estimator, (vi) the exact reflection-symmetry demonstration in 2D (a special case of hard-constraint design). The title, abstract and conclusions were changed accordingly.

> **Major (ii).** The initial condition of the 1D problem does not satisfy the homogeneous Neumann condition (u_x(−1,0)=2).

Correct (u₀′(∓1)=±2). That initial condition is the standard benchmark of the *periodic* Allen–Cahn problem, and we had used it with Neumann conditions. We replaced it by u₀ = ½(cos πx − cos²πx), which has the same values at x=0, ±½, ±1 and u₀′(±1)=0 (Section 2.1, Eq. 5) and **repeated all experiments** with it, against a new certified reference solution (Appendix E). Appendix D repeats the key experiments with the original condition. **TODO: one sentence on what Appendix D shows (expected: the same conclusions).**

> **Major (iii).** The Lean-verified results are not connected to a stability or convergence analysis of the Spectral-PINN; Theorem A1 assumes an exact gradient flow which the trained network does not satisfy.

Agreed. The abstract Hilbert-space theorem indeed says nothing about a trained network, and we now say so explicitly (Sections 2.9, 4.3). We replaced the connection that was missing by an *a-posteriori energy balance for an arbitrary trajectory*: for u = Σ c_k(t)T_k(x) with residual r = u_t − ε²u_xx + f(u), dE/dt = −‖u_t‖² + ⟨r,u_t⟩ + ε²[u_x u_t]₋₁¹ (Eq. 10, Proposition B.5), hence E(t) + ½∫‖u_t‖² ≤ E(s) + ½∫‖r‖² + ∫β. The identity, its inequalities, the integrated and the discrete-time versions and the integration by parts for polynomials are machine-checked in Lean 4 (Appendix B, Table B1; 41 theorems, no `sorry`, standard axioms only). The result is conditional on the residual and the boundary defect, and we **measure both for every trained network** (Section 3.4, Table 5): e.g. the time-integrated ½‖r‖² is 1.3·10⁻⁵ for the revised K=32 network against an energy decrease of 0.071, i.e. the energy of the learned solution cannot increase by more than 0.02 % of its decrease; with the exact Neumann basis the boundary defect vanishes identically. We do not claim a convergence theorem for the trained network; this would need, besides this balance, a stability estimate of the PDE with respect to the residual (Section 4.3).

> **3.** The two curves of Figure 1 coincide; what should the reader conclude?

That the penalty is inactive in this regime. The unpenalized network already decreases its energy at every interval of grids of spacing 0.02, 0.002 and 2·10⁻⁴; the energy decreases by at least 1.3·10⁻³ per interval of length 0.02 while the two curves differ by at most 2.2·10⁻⁶, and the penalty term is positive in at most one of 6000 epochs. The statement of the first version that the penalty "acts as a certificate" and "keeps enforcing zero violations" is withdrawn. The figure (now Figure 3) shows the difference of the curves on a logarithmic scale and the dependence on λ_E.

> **4.** The classical Chebyshev solver is faster and more accurate; state the intended niche.

Agreed, and the numbers are in Table 1: on B1 classical collocation (N=80, δt=5·10⁻⁴) has L² = 5.4·10⁻⁵ in 0.2 s against 8.3·10⁻⁴ (and minutes of training) for the revised Spectral-PINN, and a diffusion-free ODE already has L² = 1.9·10⁻³ on that benchmark, i.e. B1 hardly tests the PDE. We therefore added a diffusion-active benchmark (B2: ε=0.1) and an interface benchmark (B3: ε=0.05, T=5), stronger PINN baselines (L-BFGS stage) and the classical solver on all benchmarks. Section 4.1 states that the method is *not* a competitor for the forward problem, says what the experiments do establish (a gain over the vanilla PINN, exact structure at no cost, a computable energy certificate) and lists the settings in which such networks could be useful but which we have not tested.

> **5.** The ≈38 % bias in the recovery of ε is attributed to loss weighting, warm-up or observation design, but no ablation is given.

See A.2. The ablation is now provided (Section 3.5, Tables 6–8): the estimate is insensitive to the data weight, the number of observation points and snapshots and to K because it is determined by the starting value (ε̂/ε_init ∈ [0.61, 0.73] for ε_init/ε_true ∈ [0.5, 4], Table 6). The replacement estimator (variable projection with a data-fitting phase) recovers ε with relative errors of **TODO: inv.vp_err_005 / inv.vp_err_01** on noise-free data at ε = 0.05 and 0.1; at ε = 0.01 the data carry too little information (‖∂d/∂log ε‖ = 0.022; with σ = 0.01 the Cramér–Rao standard error is 45 %), so the inverse problem of the first version at ε = 0.01 was not meaningful.
